## Central question and mechanism

The paper asks whether decentralized competition for mobile production can cause local governments to choose positive climate protection even when a coordinated planner prefers no additional protection, once firms can respond by changing geographic backup readiness.

The central mechanism links:

1. public protection lowering primary disruption risk;
2. safer primary production lowering the private return to costly geographic redundancy;
3. endogenous redundancy crowd-out reducing the national marginal value of additional protection;
4. unilateral protection attracting mobile primary production;
5. local plant-attraction benefits keeping the decentralized marginal incentive positive.

## Material assumptions that must be understood as restrictions

- two jurisdictions and two differentiated-product firms;
- baseline linear local-risk technology;
- quadratic backup-readiness cost;
- differentiated Cournot product-market competition;
- conditional independence of backup success given primary failures;
- additive private location-fit heterogeneity;
- equal one-half incidence of national product-market surplus in local objectives;
- quadratic public-protection cost and simultaneous local policy choice.

The v2.4 diagnostics relax only the linear risk technology and the Uniform location-shock distribution. They do not establish a theorem for arbitrary backup technology, market competition, dependence structure, incidence rule, or timing.
