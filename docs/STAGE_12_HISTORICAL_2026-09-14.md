## 1. Why a RAND attempt is defensible

The manuscript contains an IO mechanism rather than merely adding climate language to a public-finance model:

- disaster-state monopoly versus duopoly profits generate a scarcity-rent wedge;
- that wedge makes backup readiness strategically substitutable;
- product substitutability changes private resilience incentives;
- endogenous firm location interacts with local public-input competition;
- the full game changes the ranking of coordinated and decentralized protection.

The general marginal identity

`M_L = M_S/4 + 2 b lambda_0`

also converts the exact witness into an economically transparent decentralization wedge. The exact witness remains necessary for the global existence theorem, but the mechanism itself is not tied to that rational parameter vector.

RAND's current stated scope covers theoretical and empirical industrial organization and closely related applied microeconomics, including organizations and regulation. Recent RAND work also includes market-structure questions in energy and certification settings, so the climate/resilience application is not inherently outside scope.

## 2. Why RAND remains a stretch

The likely editorial objections are substantive rather than technical:

1. **Importance/general reach.** The full policy-ranking reversal is new, but every individual ingredient has prior art.
2. **Parametric baseline.** The global theorem uses a two-firm differentiated Cournot model with a reduced-form backup technology and an exact canonical witness.
3. **Quantitative relevance.** The paper establishes possibility and mechanism, not the empirical frequency or magnitude of the conflict.
4. **Field identity.** An editor could view the paper as environmental/public economics with IO ingredients rather than a central IO contribution.
5. **Institutional payoff specification.** The local plant-benefit term is intentionally reduced-form; a RAND referee may ask how broadly the result survives richer fiscal incidence.

These are not reasons to reopen the theory before submission. They are reasons to treat RAND as a low-probability, high-upside first shot.

## 3. Closest-literature kill check

The claim boundary from Stage 11C survives the current re-check.

- Grames et al. (2019) already show that existing/public flood defense can induce a firm to locate closer to water and invest less in private flood defense.
- Zhao, Yang & Zhang (2026) provide quasi-experimental evidence that public flood protection crowds out private defensive investment and increases tail-risk losses.
- Meng, Ding & Wang (2024) connect local-government competition to industrial resilience through market segmentation and industrial-structure distortions.
- The classic local-public-input and endogenous-location literature already supplies the plant-attraction competition component.

None of these papers, in the checked material, contains the same full-game result in which public protection changes firms' geographic production redundancy, disaster-state oligopoly profits feed back into that redundancy, and decentralized competition for mobile production reverses the coordinated policy ranking.

The novelty claim must remain exactly that narrow.

## 4. Journal sequence

### 1. RAND Journal of Economics — one-shot stretch

Best case if the editor views disaster-state market structure and organizational resilience as a general IO mechanism. High desk-reject risk because the application is specialized and the quantitative importance is not established.

### 2. Journal of Environmental Economics and Management — immediate fallback

Best substantive fit. JEEM explicitly welcomes climate-change research and work at the intersection of environmental economics and industrial organization. The paper clears the threshold of being more than a routine extension: the full-game policy reversal, exact global certification, and marginal decomposition are the core case for broad interest.

### 3. International Journal of Industrial Organization — strong IO fallback

Good fit if the paper is presented primarily as strategic investment/organization under disaster-state market power and regulation rather than as climate policy alone.

### 4. Regional Science and Urban Economics — strong spatial-policy fallback

Very natural fit for endogenous location, local public protection, interjurisdictional competition, and spatial resilience. Lower upside than RAND/JEEM but excellent thematic fit.

### Conditional alternative: Journal of Public Economics

Potentially viable if the paper is reframed more strongly around fiscal federalism, local public-good competition, and the planner/local-government wedge. In the current manuscript JEEM is the more natural immediate fallback because climate adaptation and private resilience are essential rather than incidental.

The Journal of Urban Economics is less natural because the current model has jurisdictions and location but no specifically urban mechanism such as land, housing, commuting, density, or agglomeration.
