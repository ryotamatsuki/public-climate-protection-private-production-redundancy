## 1. Stage-7.5A / v2.4 certified contribution profile

The ranking below consumes, rather than renegotiates, the v2.4 certificate.

### Headline result

**Classification: CONDITIONALLY PORTABLE**

Within the maintained symmetric primitive family, a nonempty open set exists in which the coordinated planner chooses zero additional local protection while decentralized jurisdictions sustain a strictly positive symmetric protection equilibrium.

The mechanism is:

1. public protection lowers primary disruption risk;
2. safer primary production lowers firms' private return to geographic backup readiness;
3. endogenous redundancy crowd-out can make the national marginal value of additional protection negative;
4. unilateral protection attracts mobile primary production;
5. the local plant-attraction rent can preserve a positive decentralized marginal incentive.

Two pre-specified diagnostic alternatives—nonlinear risk technology and logistic location heterogeneity—preserve the canonical planner-zero / local-positive ranking, but exact global proof remains baseline-specific.

### Supporting claims

- public-protection / private-redundancy substitution: **CONDITIONALLY PORTABLE**;
- plant-attraction response under alternative heterogeneity: **CONDITIONALLY PORTABLE**;
- exact identity `M_L = M_S/4 + 2 b lambda_0`: **INSTITUTION-SPECIFIC** in the 1/4 coefficient;
- no novelty is claimed for adaptation crowd-out, plant attraction, local-public-input competition, or strategic substitution separately.

### Maximum defensible contribution wording

The paper establishes a new global policy-ranking conflict in a strategic mobile-oligopoly climate-protection model, supported by exact global certificates and two pre-specified portability diagnostics. It does not establish that the theorem holds under arbitrary risk technologies, market structures, backup technologies, incidence rules, or heterogeneity distributions.

## 3. Current journal evidence

### Environmental and Resource Economics — selected Primary

Official scope accessed 2026-09-25:
https://link.springer.com/journal/10640

ERE describes its primary concern as the application of economic theory and methods to environmental issues and highlights environmental-policy instruments, modeling/simulation, institutional arrangements, and policy formulation.

This is unusually close to the paper's actual structure: a formal model of climate protection, firm adaptation, mobile production, decentralized policy, and welfare.

Recent publication evidence confirms a live theory audience:

- Martín-Herrán, Martínez-García and Morales (2026), *The impacts of environmental policy on industrial allocation: A transboundary pollution dynamic game*, ERE 89, Article 24.
- Elgersma (2025), *Mitigation, Adaptation and Cooperation in Response to Climate Disaster*, ERE 88, 3187–3214.
- Eisenack (2014), *The inefficiency of private adaptation to pollution in the presence of endogenous market structure*, ERE 57, 81–99.

The paper's climate-adaptation framing is therefore not being forced onto a journal that mainly expects empirical identification.

**Fit: VERY STRONG.**  
**Primary risk:** the editor may view the exact global theorem as too stylized or the component mechanisms as too familiar.  
**Why the v2.4 strength is adequate:** ERE's demonstrated theory portfolio does not require a universal theorem; careful model-specific theory with policy content is within scope.

### Journal of Environmental Economics and Management — Stretch

Official scope accessed 2026-09-25:
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-environmental-economics-and-management/0095-0696

JEEM explicitly welcomes theoretical environmental economics, climate change, firm/government behavior, and intersections with industrial organization and urban economics. It also states that theory must be insightful, novel, and of broad interest, and that it does not publish theoretical analyses that merely extend well-known models.

Recent examples include:

- *The Political Economy of (Lacking) Commitment to Green Policies* (2025), a theory contribution;
- *Hot and cold choices: The role of extreme temperatures in shaping industrial geographical distribution* (2026), directly linking climate risk to industrial geography.

**Fit: STRONG BUT STRETCH.**  
**Desk/referee risk:** v2.2 establishes that several ingredients are known, so the editor must value the full-game policy reversal as broad rather than merely clever.  
**Decision:** keep as a stretch option, but do not chase JEEM by strengthening claims beyond the v2.4 certificate.

### Resource and Energy Economics — Realistic fallback

Official scope accessed 2026-09-25:
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/resource-and-energy-economics/0928-7655

The journal explicitly welcomes theoretical environmental/resource economics, climate change adaptation, policy instruments, and mechanism-based analysis.

Recent examples:

https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/international-journal-of-industrial-organization/0167-7187

IJIO welcomes theoretical work on strategic behavior, market structure, internal organization, and regulation. Recent work such as *Supply chain disruption and precautionary industrial policy* (2026) shows a live resilience/disruption audience.

EEPS has recently published noncooperative theoretical environmental-policy work with imperfect competition. These outlets do not require weakening the certified contribution but offer a narrower/lower-risk audience if broader field journals reject.

## 5. Explicit exclusions

### RAND Journal of Economics

The historical RAND-first plan is superseded. RAND's scope is compatible with IO, but the current contribution certificate and v2.2 absorption record make a RAND-first strategy poorly calibrated. The paper would face a high importance/general-reach burden unrelated to a mathematical defect. No theory reopening is justified merely to regain RAND fit.

### Journal of Public Economics

The paper has a genuine decentralization wedge, and Markusen et al. (1995) creates a closest-literature link. But the exact decentralization coefficient is institution-specific and the substantive new mechanism is climate protection interacting with private resilience and disaster-state competition. A JPubE-first strategy would over-weight the public-finance component.

### Journal of Urban Economics

No urban mechanism—land, housing, commuting, density, agglomeration, city structure—is central to the model.

### Journal of Environmental Economics and Policy

Current aims explicitly state that theoretical analyses are not published. Scope exclusion.

### International Tax and Public Finance

Walz-Wellisch (1996) makes the venue relevant as a parent-literature cross-check, but the present policy instrument is not tax/fiscal policy and the novel mechanism is environmental/resilience based.
