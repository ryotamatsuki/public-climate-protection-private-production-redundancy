## 1. Application-neutral canonical object

Strip climate/application labels. The candidate is a sequential game with:

1. two jurisdictions choosing a risk-reducing local public input;
2. two differentiated-product firms making endogenous binary primary-location choices;
3. firms choosing continuous costly contingency readiness after location;
4. correlated primary availability when co-located;
5. a downstream oligopoly state game;
6. a coordinated planner who values national industrial surplus and public-input cost;
7. decentralized governments who internalize a fixed share of national surplus plus a
   local hosting benefit.

The headline theorem is an **existence theorem on a nonempty open set**: the coordinated
protection-policy optimum is zero while a strictly positive symmetric local-policy Nash
equilibrium exists.

## 2. Newly added parent-class literature

### Kousky, Luttmer & Zeckhauser (2006)

**Private Investment and Government Protection**, *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty*
33(1--2), 73--100, DOI 10.1007/s11166-006-0172-y.

Mapping:
- public disaster protection ↔ local protection;
- private investment in risk-prone locations ↔ endogenous private capital/location;
- feedback between protection and investment ↔ endogenous policy-benefit feedback.

What it absorbs:
- the broad claim that public disaster protection and private location/investment are
  jointly determined;
- the idea that better public protection can attract private capital;
- the need to distinguish local first-order conditions from global welfare when protection
  benefits become non-concave and multiple equilibria can arise.

What it does not absorb:
- it explicitly sets aside complementarity/substitutability between public and private
  protective investment;
- it does not model firms' endogenous geographic contingency readiness;
- it does not contain two competing jurisdictions attracting mobile oligopolistic
  production;
- it does not generate or prove the PCPPR planner-zero / local-positive policy ranking.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ABSORBED / NOT A PARENT THEOREM FOR THE HEADLINE RESULT.**

### Mahmud & Barbier (2016)

**Are Private Defensive Expenditures against Storm Damages Affected by Public Programs
and Natural Barriers?**, *Environment and Development Economics* 21(6), 767--788,
DOI 10.1017/S1355770X16000164.

Mapping:
- public physical barriers/programs ↔ public protection;
- household self-protection/self-insurance ↔ private protective responses.

What it absorbs:
- broad public/private disaster-protection interaction;
- the possibility of crowd-in or crowd-out depending on the private margin.

What it does not absorb:
- mobile productive-firm location;
- oligopoly availability;
- local-government competition for plants;
- the full coordinated/decentralized policy-ranking theorem.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY ABSORBED.**

### Lee & Pinto (2009)

**Crime in a Multi-Jurisdictional Model with Private and Public Prevention**,
*Journal of Regional Science* 49(5), 977--996, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9787.2009.00619.x.

Application-neutral mapping:
- jurisdictional public prevention ↔ local protective input;
- private prevention ↔ private defensive action;
- spatial response to jurisdictional protection ↔ endogenous spatial behavior.

What it absorbs:
- a multi-jurisdiction public/private prevention parent class with spatial responses.

What it does not absorb:
- mobile production and local hosting benefits;
- geographic backup readiness for production;
- the disaster-state oligopoly continuation;
- the headline policy ranking.

**Verdict: STRUCTURAL PARENT / NOT DIRECT ABSORPTION.**

### Hickey et al. (2021)

**Private Protection and Public Policing**, *Journal of Public Economic Theory* 23(1),
5--28, DOI 10.1111/jpet.12473.

Mapping:
- public policing ↔ public protection;
- private protection ↔ private defensive investment.

What it absorbs:
- public/private protection complementarity/substitution as a general strategic object.

What it does not absorb:
- disaster risk, plant mobility, endogenous geographic redundancy, oligopoly availability,
  or interjurisdictional plant-attraction competition.

**Verdict: STRUCTURAL PARENT / NOT DIRECT ABSORPTION.**

## 4. Strongest combined absorption attack

The strongest challenge is not any single paper but the combination:

1. **Kousky et al.** supplies government protection ↔ private risky-location investment;
2. **Mahmud--Barbier / Hickey et al.** supply public/private protection interaction;
3. **Walz--Wellisch / Maurer--Walz** supply strategic local public-input competition for
   mobile firms;
4. **Grossman et al.** supplies private geographic resilience distortions under imperfect
   competition.

Together these works contain most ingredients.

Under workflow v2.5, “no one paper combines all ingredients” is not sufficient novelty.
The relevant question is whether an existing parent theorem makes the headline result a
direct specialization or short corollary.

No such parent theorem was identified. The prior results do not jointly imply, by a
short mapping alone, that a risk-reducing local public input can have **negative national
industrial marginal value because firms reduce geographic contingency readiness** while
remaining privately attractive to local governments, nor do they supply the global
planner/Nash certificate used here.
