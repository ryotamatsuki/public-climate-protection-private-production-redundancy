\large Local Resilience Competition, Plant Location, and Disaster-State Market Power}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
Climate protection makes production sites safer, but firms can respond by reducing geographic backup readiness and changing primary locations. This paper studies two jurisdictions competing for mobile differentiated-product firms through protection against localized disruption. Firms choose locations and costly backup readiness before disasters and product-market competition. Protection has a positive direct reliability benefit. Nevertheless, private readiness can fail to capture its full social value, so induced backup reductions can outweigh that benefit. Within the maintained symmetric primitive family, a nonempty open set has a unique zero optimum for a coordinated protection-policy planner and a strictly positive symmetric decentralized equilibrium. The benchmark covers industrial surplus and resilience costs, conditional on private firm choices; it excludes protection benefits to lives and other assets. A marginal decomposition isolates the plant-attraction incentive that sustains local spending after the national industrial marginal value becomes negative. An exact benchmark shows that this ranking does not require disaster-state incremental monopoly rents or strategic backup substitution. Those oligopoly effects instead alter readiness and its dependence on correlated failures. The result identifies when industrial adaptation appraisal must account jointly for the private--social readiness wedge, endogenous resilience responses, and competition for primary production.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} climate adaptation; private resilience; firm location; public investment; oligopoly; fiscal competition.\\
