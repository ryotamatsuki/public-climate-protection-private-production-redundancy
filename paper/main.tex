\title{\textbf{Public Climate Protection and Private Production Redundancy}\\
\large Local Resilience Competition, Plant Location, and Disaster-State Market Power}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Local climate-protection investments can make production sites safer, but firms respond by changing where they locate and how much geographic backup capacity they maintain. This paper studies two jurisdictions that compete for mobile oligopolistic production by investing in local climate protection. Firms first choose primary production locations and then choose costly backup readiness in another location before localized disasters occur and surviving firms compete in a differentiated product market. Public protection lowers local disruption risk but crowds out private geographic redundancy. The paper's contribution is not either force in isolation. Their interaction creates a policy-ranking conflict: within the maintained symmetric primitive family, there is a nonempty open set in which coordinated welfare is maximized by zero additional local protection while the decentralized jurisdictional game has a strictly positive symmetric protection equilibrium. A general marginal decomposition shows that the local incentive equals one quarter of the national marginal value plus a plant-attraction term, clarifying when decentralized protection remains attractive after the national marginal value becomes negative. Product-market substitutability strengthens the disaster-state rent channel that governs private redundancy. The results imply that place-based resilience investment should be evaluated jointly with firms' endogenous geographic production portfolios.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} climate adaptation; resilience; firm location; public investment; oligopoly; geographic redundancy; fiscal competition.\\
