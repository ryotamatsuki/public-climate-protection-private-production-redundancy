\section{Welfare and the Public--Private Resilience Substitution}\label{sec:welfare}

\subsection{Exact surplus accounting}

From utility \eqref{eq:utility}, consumer surplus when both firms survive is
\begin{equation}
CS_D=\frac{1+\gamma}{(2+\gamma)^2}.
\end{equation}
With total producer surplus $2/(2+\gamma)^2$, total surplus in the duopoly state is
\begin{equation}
T_D=\frac{3+\gamma}{(2+\gamma)^2}.
\label{eq:TD}
\end{equation}
When only one firm survives,
\begin{equation}
CS_M=\frac18,\qquad PS_M=\frac14,\qquad T_M=\frac38.
\label{eq:TM}
\end{equation}

Let
\begin{equation}
m_i=s_i(1-r_i)
\end{equation}
be firm $i$'s probability of being unavailable. Under the conditional-independence backup-success law in Section~\ref{sec:model}, the probability that both firms are unavailable is
\begin{equation}
z=J(1-r_1)(1-r_2).
\end{equation}
The four final availability probabilities are therefore $1-m_1-m_2+z$, $m_2-z$, $m_1-z$, and $z$. Expected product-market surplus net of backup costs is
\begin{equation}
S
=T_D+(T_M-T_D)(m_1+m_2)+(T_D-2T_M)z
-\frac{k}{2}(r_1^2+r_2^2).
\label{eq:surplus}
\end{equation}
To connect this state-level accounting to the policy problem, let $S^{XY}(a_A,a_B)$ denote \eqref{eq:surplus} after solving the backup continuation when firm 1 locates in $X\in\{A,B\}$ and firm 2 in $Y\in\{A,B\}$. With the common equilibrium location probability $p$ from \eqref{eq:location-prob}, expected national market surplus is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S(a_A,a_B)
=
p^2 S^{AA}
+p(1-p)S^{AB}
+p(1-p)S^{BA}
+(1-p)^2S^{BB}.
\label{eq:expected-surplus-location}
\end{equation}
Substituting the profile-specific backup solutions from \eqref{eq:backup-linear-system} and the location probability \eqref{eq:location-prob} into \eqref{eq:expected-surplus-location}, and then substituting the result into \eqref{eq:government-payoff} and \eqref{eq:national-welfare}, produces the rational policy functions whose exact signs are certified in Appendix~\ref{app:verification}. The welfare comparison therefore reflects real output losses, consumer surplus, producer surplus, and real public and private resilience costs. It is not generated by treating fiscal transfers as social costs.

\subsection{The social--private readiness wedge}

Write $S(s_1,s_2,J,r_1,r_2)$ for the profile surplus in \eqref{eq:surplus} before readiness is optimized. Differentiating and substituting the interior private FOC gives
\begin{align}
S_{r_i}
&=(T_D-T_M)s_i+(2T_M-T_D)J(1-r_j)-kr_i,\nonumber\\
\Omega_i\equiv S_{r_i}\big|_{\mathrm{private\ FOC}}
&=\frac{2-3\gamma}{8(2+\gamma)}s_i
+\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}J(1-r_j).
\label{eq:readiness-wedge}
\end{align}
The first coefficient reflects the social value of restoring the firm's product beyond the private duopoly payoff. The second adjusts the value of recovery in joint-failure states relative to the incremental private sole-survivor return. At the canonical $\gamma=12/25$, both coefficients are positive. Firms therefore leave a positive social marginal value of additional readiness at their private optimum. A reduction in readiness loses surplus after its cost savings have been deducted. At $\gamma=0$, the wedge is still $s_i/8>0$; incremental disaster-state market power is not required. For $\gamma>2/3$ the first coefficient is negative, so no all-parameter underinvestment claim follows from this formula.

Along the symmetric policy path $p=1/2$, and the four location weights remain $1/4$. At the origin, the chain rule therefore yields
\begin{equation}
M_S=E_0+\frac14\sum_{XY}\sum_{i=1}^2
\Omega_i^{XY}\frac{dr_i^{XY}}{da}.
\label{eq:readiness-chain-rule}
\end{equation}
Here $E_0$ holds readiness fixed. Under the baseline risk law it is strictly positive for interior readiness: lower common primary risk increases expected production availability, while public marginal cost is zero at the origin. The second term is $R_0$ in Table~\ref{tab:channels}. If each profile's readiness instead satisfied the interior social condition $S_{r_i}=0$, this term would vanish and $M_S=E_0>0$. Thus an inefficient private substitute, not substitution alone, is essential to the negative origin marginal. Correcting backup incentives and coordinating protection are distinct policy interventions.

\subsection{Why product-market competition matters}

The scarcity-rent wedge $\Delta(\gamma)$ links the industrial-organization block to the resilience decision. A higher $\gamma$ lowers duopoly profit relative to monopoly profit, thereby increasing $\Delta$. Equations \eqref{eq:rC}--\eqref{eq:rD} then imply that equilibrium backup readiness falls with product substitutability on the maintained symmetric interior branch. This does not mean that competition always lowers resilience in richer environments, nor does it assign a general sign to the interaction between product substitutability and the marginal response of readiness to protection. In the maintained baseline model, backup readiness is valuable partly because it determines who remains active after a localized disruption, so the relative value of competitive survival versus sole survival is an essential determinant of the firm's private resilience incentive.
