\section{Nested Benchmarks}\label{sec:benchmarks}

The contribution depends on an interaction, so the appropriate comparison is not whether each component is individually familiar. It is whether the policy regime in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} survives after either strategic margin is removed. Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks} summarizes the nested games.

\subsection{Fixed primary locations}

If primary locations are fixed, a unilateral protection increase cannot attract a primary plant. The $2bp$ term in \eqref{eq:government-payoff} no longer responds to $a_A$. Public protection can still crowd out private backup readiness, but the specific wedge that sustains positive local protection after the coordinated marginal value turns negative disappears.

\subsection{Fixed private redundancy}

If backup readiness is fixed, local protection affects primary risk and plant attraction but cannot reorganize firms' private resilience portfolios. The model then collapses toward the familiar regional-competition problem in which local public inputs are used to compete for mobile production. The central theorem is unavailable because the planner's policy ranking cannot be reversed by an endogenous reduction in private backup capacity.

\subsection{Full-game interaction}

The full model combines the two margins but does more than place them side by side. Public protection changes the private resilience architecture, which changes the national value of additional protection. Local governments then act on a different objective because they value the induced relocation of primary production. The resulting policy regime is therefore generated by the interaction of two strategic responses rather than by relabeling either benchmark.
