\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Public protection changes firms' private resilience and primary locations. Where private contingency choices leave an uninternalized availability benefit, protection-induced readiness reductions can outweigh the direct reliability gain after real preparedness-cost savings are included. Local governments may still provide protection because they value attracting primary establishments.

The model establishes a nonempty open set in its symmetric baseline family in which a coordinated protection-policy planner uniquely chooses zero additional protection while a positive symmetric decentralized Nash equilibrium satisfies global unilateral incentives. This is a constrained industrial-surplus comparison. It does not cover all benefits of climate adaptation or the first-best choice of public and private resilience. The marginal decomposition $M_L=M_S/4+2b\lambda_0$ separates the national industrial value from the attraction incentive. An exact $\Delta=0$ benchmark shows that incremental disaster-state monopoly rents and strategic backup substitution are not necessary for the ranking; oligopoly changes readiness and its response to correlated availability states.

Fixing primary locations removes the attraction derivative, and fixing readiness removes its endogenous response. These interventions identify terms in the mechanism rather than establish universal absence of policy conflict in restricted games. Nonlinear-risk and logistic-location exercises provide specified numerical diagnostics, while the exact global theorem retains its baseline scope.

For industrial adaptation appraisal, the relevant objects are the direct reliability gain, the social--private readiness wedge and resulting contingency response, and the relocation incentive attached to local protection. Measuring only engineering risk reduction or treating backup as fixed can miss a negative industrial margin. Evaluating those responses jointly is also necessary before interpreting local spending as additional national resilience.
