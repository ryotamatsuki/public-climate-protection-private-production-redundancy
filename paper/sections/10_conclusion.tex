\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Public protection changes firms' private resilience and primary locations. Where private contingency choices leave an uninternalized availability benefit, protection-induced readiness reductions can outweigh the direct reliability gain after real preparedness-cost savings are included. Local governments may still provide protection because they value attracting primary establishments.

The model establishes a nonempty open set in its symmetric baseline family in which a coordinated protection-policy planner uniquely chooses zero additional protection while a positive symmetric decentralized Nash equilibrium satisfies global unilateral incentives. The comparison concerns industrial surplus conditional on private firm choices, with other benefits of adaptation outside its objective. The marginal decomposition $M_L=M_S/4+2b\lambda_0$ separates the national industrial value from the attraction incentive. An exact $\Delta=0$ benchmark shows that incremental disaster-state monopoly rents and strategic backup substitution are not necessary for the ranking; oligopoly changes readiness and its response to correlated availability states.

Fixed-location and fixed-readiness comparisons isolate the corresponding terms in the mechanism. Nonlinear-risk and logistic-location exercises give numerical diagnostics beyond the baseline; their constructional agreement at symmetry and the theorem's functional-form restrictions are stated in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

For industrial adaptation appraisal, the relevant objects are the direct reliability gain, the social--private readiness wedge and resulting contingency response, and the relocation incentive attached to local protection. Measuring only engineering risk reduction or treating backup as fixed can miss a negative industrial margin. Evaluating those responses jointly is also necessary before interpreting local spending as additional national resilience.
