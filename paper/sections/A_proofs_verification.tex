\section{Proofs and Computer-Assisted Certificates}\label{app:verification}

This appendix separates the human-readable proof from the exact computations that certify its global inequalities. The logical structure is: (i) derive the continuation games from the probability primitives; (ii) state exact lemmas for continuation regularity, planner monotonicity, and the local government's global best response; (iii) prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from those lemmas; and (iv) establish persistence on an open neighborhood of the canonical witness. The accompanying code permits the algebraic certificates to be checked, but the theorem follows from the mathematical statements recorded here rather than from a development-history label or a numerical search.

\subsection{Product-market and backup identities}

The Cournot first-order conditions from \eqref{eq:inverse-demand} are
\begin{equation}
1-2x_i-\gamma x_j=0.
\end{equation}
Symmetry yields $x_i=1/(2+\gamma)$ and \eqref{eq:duopoly}; the monopoly result \eqref{eq:monopoly} is immediate. Total product-market surplus is $(3+\gamma)/(2+\gamma)^2$ when both firms are available and $3/8$ when exactly one is available.

Let $m_i=s_i(1-r_i)$ be firm $i$'s unavailability probability. Under the conditional-independence primitive in Section~\ref{sec:model}, joint unavailability is
\begin{equation}
z=J(1-r_1)(1-r_2).
\end{equation}
Hence the probabilities of the four final availability states are
\begin{align}
\Pr(1,1)&=1-m_1-m_2+z,\\
\Pr(1,0)&=m_2-z,\\
\Pr(0,1)&=m_1-z,\\
\Pr(0,0)&=z.
\end{align}
Multiplying these probabilities by the relevant duopoly or monopoly profits gives \eqref{eq:expected-profit}. For fixed $r_j$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\Pi_i}{\partial r_i}
=\pi_D s_i+\Delta J(1-r_j)-kr_i,
\qquad
\frac{\partial^2\Pi_i}{\partial r_i^2}=-k<0,
\end{equation}
so \eqref{eq:backup-br-global} is the global best response on $[0,1]$.

\begin{lemma}[Unique interior continuation at the canonical witness]\label{lem:continuation}
For the parameter vector in \eqref{eq:witness} and every policy pair $(a_A,a_B)\in[0,2/25]^2$, each location profile induces a unique interior backup Nash equilibrium. The subsequent private-information location game is globally unclipped and has a unique interior Bayesian equilibrium in cutoff probabilities. The Cournot continuation is unique in every final availability state.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each of the four primary-location profiles, the interior backup first-order conditions form a two-equation linear system. The exact certificate verifies on the full policy rectangle that its determinant is strictly positive, that both resulting readiness levels satisfy $0<r_i<1$, and that the linear best-response slopes have product strictly below one in absolute value. Because each firm's objective is globally strictly concave in its own readiness, the interior solution is the unique global backup Nash equilibrium and no boundary solution is omitted.

For the location game, define $u_0,u_1,B$ as in \eqref{eq:location-endpoints}. Exact polynomial inequalities verify $0<u_0,u_1<1$ throughout the policy rectangle. Thus the affine cutoff best response never clips. Since $B=u_1-u_0$, these endpoint inequalities imply $|B|<1$, so the two-firm affine best-response system is a contraction. It therefore has a unique probability fixed point and cannot support a distinct asymmetric probability solution. The certificate also verifies that the denominator in \eqref{eq:location-prob} is strictly positive and that the resulting probability lies in $(0,1)$. The final Cournot games have the strictly concave quadratic objectives already solved above.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Plant-attraction decomposition}

At the symmetric origin, symmetry implies $\mathcal S_{a_A}=\mathcal S_{a_B}$, while the quadratic public-protection cost has zero marginal cost. Hence
\begin{equation}
M_S=\mathcal S_{a_A}+\mathcal S_{a_B}=2\mathcal S_{a_A}.
\end{equation}
From \eqref{eq:government-payoff},
\begin{equation}
M_L=\frac12\mathcal S_{a_A}+2b\lambda_0.
\end{equation}
Combining the two equations proves \eqref{eq:marginal-decomposition}; rearranging gives \eqref{eq:attraction-threshold}. If $M_S=E_0+R_0$ with $E_0>0$ and $R_0<0$, the pair of inequalities in \eqref{eq:transparent-conflict} is equivalent to the marginal sign conflict $M_S<0<M_L$ under this decomposition. It is a marginal characterization, not by itself a global-equilibrium theorem.

\subsection{Exact certificate method and normalization}\label{app:certificate-method}

All global sign statements below are evaluated with exact rational arithmetic. The certificate uses one elementary observation repeatedly: a weighted average of strictly negative numbers is strictly negative, provided all weights are nonnegative and sum to one. The Bernstein basis supplies exactly such weights.

To see the idea in one variable before using the general formula, consider a quadratic polynomial written as
\begin{equation}
P(x)=\beta_0(1-x)^2+\beta_1\,2x(1-x)+\beta_2x^2,
\qquad 0\leq x\leq 1.
\label{eq:bernstein-simple}
\end{equation}
The three weights $(1-x)^2$, $2x(1-x)$, and $x^2$ are all nonnegative and satisfy
\[
(1-x)^2+2x(1-x)+x^2=1.
\]
Hence $P(x)$ is a weighted average of $\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2$. If all three coefficients are strictly negative, then $P(x)<0$ for every $x\in[0,1]$; if all are strictly positive, then $P(x)>0$ everywhere. This is a whole-interval statement, not a check on a finite grid.

The same argument extends directly to the two policy variables. For a polynomial $P(a_A,a_B)$ of bidegree at most $(m,n)$, normalize the policy rectangle by setting
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{a_A}{\bar a},\qquad y=\frac{a_B}{\bar a},
\end{equation}
so $(x,y)\in[0,1]^2$, and write
\begin{equation}
P(\bar a x,\bar a y)
=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\beta_{ij}
B_{i,m}(x)B_{j,n}(y),
\qquad
B_{i,m}(x)=\binom mi x^i(1-x)^{m-i}.
\label{eq:bernstein-representation}
\end{equation}
For every $x\in[0,1]$, each $B_{i,m}(x)$ is nonnegative and $\sum_i B_{i,m}(x)=1$; the same is true in $y$. Therefore the products $B_{i,m}(x)B_{j,n}(y)$ are nonnegative and sum to one over $(i,j)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:bernstein-representation} is thus a weighted average of the coefficients $\{\beta_{ij}\}$. Consequently,
\[
\beta_{ij}<0\ \text{for all }(i,j)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P(a_A,a_B)<0
\]
throughout the full policy rectangle, and the analogous statement holds for strictly positive coefficients.

The objects used below are generally rational functions rather than polynomials. If
\[
R(a_A,a_B)=\frac{P(a_A,a_B)}{Q(a_A,a_B)},
\]
the certificate first establishes that $Q$ has a fixed nonzero sign on the entire rectangle and then applies the Bernstein argument separately to $P$ and $Q$. The sign of $R$ then follows from the signs of its numerator and denominator. Thus, for example, certifying $Q>0$ and $P<0$ on the whole rectangle proves $R<0$ everywhere. No floating-point approximation or finite-grid interpolation is used in this implication.

\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Global planner monotonicity]\label{lem:planner-global}
At the canonical witness,
\begin{equation}
W_{a_A}(a_A,a_B)<0,
\qquad
W_{a_B}(a_A,a_B)<0
\end{equation}
for every $(a_A,a_B)\in[0,2/25]^2$. Hence $(0,0)$ is the unique coordinated optimum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the unique downstream continuation from Lemma~\ref{lem:continuation} into national welfare. Differentiating the resulting rational expression gives
\[
W_{a_A}=\frac{P_A(a_A,a_B)}{Q_A(a_A,a_B)},
\qquad
W_{a_B}=\frac{P_B(a_A,a_B)}{Q_B(a_A,a_B)}
\]
for explicit polynomials $P_A,P_B,Q_A,Q_B$ with rational coefficients at the canonical witness.

The exact certificate applies the argument in Section~\ref{app:certificate-method} to these four polynomials on $[0,2/25]^2$. It verifies that the relevant Bernstein coefficients of $Q_A$ and $Q_B$ are strictly positive, so
\[
Q_A>0,\qquad Q_B>0
\]
throughout the full rectangle. It also verifies that every Bernstein coefficient of $P_A$ and $P_B$ is strictly negative, so
\[
P_A<0,\qquad P_B<0
\]
throughout the same rectangle. Therefore
\[
W_{a_A}<0,\qquad W_{a_B}<0
\]
at every feasible policy pair, including the boundary.

Starting from $(0,0)$, increasing either coordinate while holding the other fixed strictly lowers welfare. Hence no point with $a_A>0$ or $a_B>0$ can maximize $W$, and $(0,0)$ is the unique coordinated optimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Global local-government best response]\label{lem:local-global}
At the canonical witness, let $\alpha$ be the exact root in \eqref{eq:alpha-exact-interval}. Then $a_A=\alpha$ is jurisdiction $A$'s unique global best response to $a_B=\alpha$ on $[0,2/25]$, and symmetrically for jurisdiction $B$. Hence $(\alpha,\alpha)$ is a positive Nash equilibrium of the policy game.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
F(a)=G_{A,a_A}(a,a)
\]
denote the first-order condition along the symmetric policy line. Exact real-root isolation shows that $F$ has exactly one root on $[0,2/25]$, and that this root $\alpha$ lies in the rational interval in \eqref{eq:alpha-exact-interval}. Thus
\[
G_{A,a_A}(\alpha,\alpha)=0.
\]

A stationary point is not yet enough for a global best response. The remaining step is to prove strict concavity in the own policy of jurisdiction $A$. After substituting the unique downstream continuation, write
\[
G_{A,a_Aa_A}(a_A,a_B)
=
\frac{P_G(a_A,a_B)}{Q_G(a_A,a_B)}.
\]
Because $\alpha$ is represented by an isolating interval rather than by a floating-point number, the certificate lets the rival action $a_B$ range over the entire rational interval containing $\alpha$, while $a_A$ ranges over its full feasible interval $[0,2/25]$.

On that rectangle, the Bernstein certificate establishes fixed nonzero signs for the numerator and denominator which, after the recorded normalization is applied, imply
\begin{equation}
G_{A,a_Aa_A}<0.
\end{equation}
The weighted-average argument in Section~\ref{app:certificate-method} makes this a whole-rectangle inequality rather than a finite-grid check. In particular, it holds for every feasible $a_A$ when $a_B=\alpha$.

Hence $G_A(\cdot,\alpha)$ is strictly concave on the entire feasible interval. A strictly concave function has at most one stationary point, and any such point is its unique global maximizer. Since $G_{A,a_A}(\alpha,\alpha)=0$, $a_A=\alpha$ is jurisdiction $A$'s unique global best response to $a_B=\alpha$, including against both boundary actions. Symmetry gives the same conclusion for jurisdiction $B$, so $(\alpha,\alpha)$ is a positive Nash equilibrium.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at the canonical witness]
Lemma~\ref{lem:continuation} makes every downstream continuation used in a first-stage deviation unique and interior. Lemma~\ref{lem:planner-global} gives the unique coordinated optimum $(0,0)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:local-global} gives the strictly positive symmetric first-stage Nash equilibrium $(\alpha,\alpha)$. These statements establish the policy-ranking reversal at the canonical witness. Lemma~\ref{lem:open-set} below extends the result to a nonempty open set within the maintained symmetric primitive family.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Open-set persistence}

\begin{lemma}[Persistence around the canonical witness]\label{lem:open-set}
There is a neighborhood of the canonical parameter vector, within the maintained symmetric primitive family, on which the unique planner optimum remains at zero, the downstream continuation remains unique and interior for every admissible policy, and a strictly positive symmetric local-government Nash equilibrium persists.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\theta$ collect the primitives and normalize public policies by $x_j=a_j/\bar a(\theta)$, so the policy domain is the fixed compact square $[0,1]^2$ for all nearby $\theta$. At the canonical vector $\theta_0$, the exact certificates establish strict inequalities for all denominator signs, backup-interiority bounds, location no-clipping conditions, and planner derivatives on that compact square. Because the relevant rational functions are continuous jointly in $(x_A,x_B,\theta)$ wherever the certified denominators remain nonzero, compactness converts each strict pointwise certificate at $\theta_0$ into a positive uniform margin. Those margins persist for all $\theta$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $\theta_0$. Consequently, the same continuation branch remains well-defined, unique, and interior, and both planner partial derivatives remain strictly negative throughout the normalized square. The coordinated optimum therefore remains at zero.

For the local-government equilibrium, let $F(a,\theta)$ denote the symmetric first-order condition. At $(\alpha,\theta_0)$, $F=0$ and the root is simple because the certified own-policy second derivative is strictly negative. The implicit-function theorem therefore yields a unique continuous root $\alpha(\theta)$ near $\alpha$, with $0<\alpha(\theta)<\bar a(\theta)$ for nearby primitives. The strict-concavity certificate has a uniform negative margin on the compact own-policy interval when the rival action ranges over a neighborhood containing the root; continuity preserves that margin for nearby $\theta$. Hence $\alpha(\theta)$ remains the unique global best response to itself. This proves persistence of the positive symmetric Nash equilibrium and completes the nonempty-open-set part of the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Product-substitutability interaction}

Equation~\eqref{eq:gamma-r} concerns the level of readiness on the symmetric interior branch. It does not assign a general sign to the interaction between substitutability and the marginal policy response. For the canonical co-located symmetric origin, exact differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{\partial^2r^C}{\partial q\,\partial\gamma}\right|_{q=3/10,\,\gamma=12/25}
=-\frac{26550065039062500}{13482801445834729}<0.
\end{equation}
Since $q=q_0-a$, this means that the marginal reduction in readiness caused by an increase in protection is locally weaker as $\gamma$ rises at this point. No opposite global interaction claim is used in the theorem.

\subsection{Robustness to counting the selected location-fit payoff}\label{app:fit-robustness}

The baseline welfare criterion treats $\varepsilon_i$ as a non-resource location-fit shifter. A natural robustness question is whether the theorem survives if the selected fit payoff is instead counted in welfare. Let $D\in[-H,H]$ be a firm's deterministic payoff advantage of $A$ over $B$. The firm chooses $A$ when $\varepsilon_i\ge -D$. The expected selected perturbation payoff for one firm is therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{2H}\int_{-D}^{H}\varepsilon\,d\varepsilon
=\frac H4-\frac{D^2}{4H}.
\end{equation}
For the two symmetric firms the aggregate term is
\begin{equation}
R(D)=\frac H2-\frac{D^2}{2H}\leq \frac H2,
\label{eq:fit-payoff}
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $D=0$. At every symmetric policy profile, including both $(0,0)$ and $(\alpha,\alpha)$, symmetry implies $D=0$. Since baseline coordinated welfare is globally maximized at $(0,0)$ and $R(D)$ is also globally maximized there, adding $R(D)$ to coordinated welfare cannot change the planner optimum. Likewise, against $a_B=\alpha$, baseline $G_A$ is globally maximized at $a_A=\alpha$, and $R(D)$ is maximized at that same symmetric point. Adding any fixed nonnegative local share of $R(D)$ therefore leaves $a_A=\alpha$ a global best response. This robustness statement depends on that specified nonnegative fixed-share allocation of the added fit term.

\subsection{Numerical stress tests and reproducibility}\label{app:continuation}

The exact certificates establish the theorem's global quantifiers. Numerical routines are retained only as counterexample searches and implementation checks. At the canonical witness, the numerical audit evaluates unilateral government deviations on 2,001 policy points, searches the two-dimensional planner problem on a deterministic grid and from multiple continuous-optimization starting points, and re-solves downstream continuation choices after each material deviation. The separate state-enumeration evaluator constructs the four final availability probabilities directly from $s_i$, $J$, and the conditional-independence backup law rather than calling the closed-form expected-payoff expression.

The reproducibility archive identifies the exact scripts corresponding to each lemma. \texttt{scripts/verify\_global\_certificate.py} generates the whole-domain Bernstein inequalities; \texttt{scripts/verify\_policy\_certificate.py} and \texttt{scripts/verify\_symbolic.py} perform exact root isolation; \texttt{scripts/verify\_normalization.py} checks the scaling of the auxiliary coefficient archive against primitive symbolic expressions; and the numerical routines serve only as independent stress tests. No floating-point computation is used to establish Lemmas~\ref{lem:continuation}--\ref{lem:local-global}.
