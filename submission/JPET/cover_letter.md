Dear Editors of the Journal of Public Economic Theory,

Please consider my manuscript, “Public Protection and Private Resilience in Competition for Mobile Firms,” as an original research article in the Journal of Public Economic Theory.

The paper addresses a local-public-input question: can competition for mobile production sustain protective public investment even when its additional national industrial value is negative because it displaces firms' privately chosen contingency readiness? In a sequential game of two jurisdictions and two differentiated-product firms, public protection affects both primary-plant location and geographic backup readiness before production disruptions and Cournot competition. The analysis distinguishes direct reliability benefits from the private–social readiness wedge and the incentive to attract primary establishments.

Beyond the original global equilibrium existence result, the revision provides explicit primitive conditions guaranteeing the policy conflict in the separable-product economy, together with an exact positive-coordinated-optimum example. A more discriminating result concerns an additional public-protection benefit: within an identified parameter region, the gap between decentralized and coordinated protection increases as that benefit grows. Fully re-solved counterfactual policy games show that the slope is positive with endogenous private readiness and firm location, zero when readiness is held fixed, and negative when locations are fixed. A compact-domain continuity theorem shows that positive-optimum overprovision and the positive comparative static survive for a nonempty set of strictly positive product-substitutability values. This is not a universal comparative-static claim across all oligopoly primitives.

The principal result is intentionally limited. Within a nonempty open set of the maintained symmetric model family, the coordinated protection-policy planner has a unique zero optimum while the decentralized game has a strictly positive symmetric Nash equilibrium with globally optimal unilateral responses. An exact benchmark shows that incremental disaster-state sole-survivor rents and strategic substitution between firms' backup choices are not required for this result. The planner coordinates the public instrument only and values industrial surplus, not the full social benefits of protection.

Our claim is not that public-input overprovision, plant attraction, or public–private protection substitution is new. We position the paper explicitly against Keen and Marchand (1997), Bayindir-Upmann (1998), Matsumoto (1998), Walz and Wellisch (1996), Maurer and Walz (2000), Billon (2023), Hickey et al. (2021), Grossman et al. (2023), and related recent work. The specific contribution is the globally certified policy-ranking conflict under endogenous private production resilience and mobile firms.

This combination of local public goods, market failure, private investment incentives, interjurisdictional competition, and game-theoretic policy analysis aligns with JPET's scope. The paper includes transparent computer-assisted exact certificates, an explicit account of the boundaries of partial formal verification, and a data/code availability statement.
