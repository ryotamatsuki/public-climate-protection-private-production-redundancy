\begin{abstract}
Can competition for mobile firms induce excessive public protection when greater reliability crowds out private resilience? We study a sequential game in which two jurisdictions choose risk-reducing public investments and firms choose primary locations and costly contingency readiness before local disruptions and Cournot competition. Public protection makes a jurisdiction more attractive but reduces private incentives to maintain backup production. We establish a nonempty open parameter region in which the coordinated policy optimum is zero, whereas decentralized governments choose strictly positive protection in a symmetric Nash equilibrium. This conflict arises because induced losses in privately chosen readiness can outweigh direct industrial reliability gains while jurisdictions still benefit from attracting primary plants. When a direct nonindustrial benefit of protection is included, the coordinated optimum can be positive yet remain below decentralized provision. In an exactly solved benchmark, increasing that benefit widens the policy gap when readiness and location are both endogenous, leaves it unchanged when readiness is fixed, and narrows it when locations are fixed. The full-game policy ranking and benefit-gap response persist locally with positive product substitutability. The welfare comparison concerns industrial surplus, not all benefits of disaster protection.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} local public inputs; interjurisdictional competition; private resilience; public--private substitution; firm location; production availability.\\
