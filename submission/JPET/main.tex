\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.} Can competition for mobile production sustain local public protection even when additional protection reduces national industrial surplus?
\textbf{Methods.} Two jurisdictions choose risk-reducing public inputs before differentiated-product firms select primary locations and costly contingency readiness; surviving producers then compete after localized disruptions.
\textbf{Results.} Public protection directly improves primary reliability but can crowd out private readiness whose social value exceeds the return internalized by firms. In a nonempty open set of the maintained symmetric primitive family, a coordinated protection-policy planner uniquely chooses zero additional provision while a strictly positive symmetric decentralized Nash equilibrium satisfies global unilateral incentives. A marginal decomposition identifies the plant-attraction motive that sustains local provision after the national industrial marginal value turns negative. An exact benchmark establishes that incremental disaster-state monopoly rents and strategic substitution between firms' backup choices are not necessary for this ranking. Exact restricted-policy games identify the readiness and relocation channels. Primitive sufficient conditions yield a positive coordinated optimum below decentralized provision. A larger direct protection benefit widens the overprovision gap when both private responses operate, but not when either response is fixed; the effect persists for small positive product substitutability and modest endogenous regional incidence and hosting variations. At the separable-product limit, stronger product competition also strictly magnifies the policy gap.
\textbf{Conclusions.} The planner coordinates only the public input, leaving firms' decisions decentralized; the welfare benchmark covers industrial surplus and resilience costs, not all benefits of disaster protection.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} local public inputs; interjurisdictional competition; private resilience; public--private substitution; firm location; production availability.\\
