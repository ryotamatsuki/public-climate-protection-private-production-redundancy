## Novelty stress test
The broad results “local industrial public inputs can be overprovided” and “public and private protection can substitute” are already established. Keen–Marchand (1997), Bayindir-Upmann (1998), Walz–Wellisch (1996), Maurer–Walz (2000), Billon (2023), Hickey et al. (2021) and Grossman–Helpman–Lhuillier (2023) establish major components. **The novelty claim must NOT be any of these individual phenomena.**

The sharper new claim is a *slope sign reversal under removal of either private response*, within the same welfare-accounting framework: for the exact gamma=0 witness, d(aN−aP)/dmu = +16/189 with both endogenous readiness and mobility, =0 with backup held fixed (mobile firms), =−16/1593 when the location distribution is fixed (readiness re-optimized). All three are full policy equilibria, not partial derivatives holding an off-path government response constant. At the original rational primitives, a larger common direct benefit paradoxically amplifies government overinvestment *only in the original joint-response game*, whereas that conclusion does not follow from a generic local-public-input competition slogan. Positive-gap response persists for some gamma>0, so it is not exclusively a zero-substitutability artifact.

The primitive conditions and the positive-gamma continuation theorem materially increase economic content beyond a parameter-specific exact witness. They map cost-adjusted reliability and plant attraction to an equilibrium ranking without claiming broad necessity.

The nearest parent Bayindir-Upmann (1998) is characterized in authoritative fiscal-competition literature as industrial public inputs with mobile *capital* but without mobile *firms*. Neither its official abstract nor the accessible secondary model descriptions include privately chosen contingency readiness. Keen–Marchand (1997) concerns the composition of public spending, not a private backup-readiness margin. Matsumoto (1998) can instead yield underprovision with mobile firms in a different tax-competition economy. None is currently demonstrated to contain the new three-way private-response derivative claim as a theorem or direct corollary. HOWEVER, the published full Bayindir-Upmann proofs have not been accessed, so exclusion is NOT finally certified; missing article access is recorded as an open literature issue.
