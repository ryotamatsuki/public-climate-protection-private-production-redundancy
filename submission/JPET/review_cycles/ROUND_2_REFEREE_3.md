# Round 2 — Referee 3: Economic mechanism and interpretation

**Simulated adversarial public-economics/industrial-organization review, 2026-11-05.**

## How the contribution changed
The initial model convincingly demonstrated a negative industrial value of public protection due to privately suboptimal disaster readiness; however, generic decentralized public-input overinvestment and public/private substitution were not new. Round 1 added a positive-planner-optimum case and two fully solved restricted-policy games.

This round provides a *more discriminating economic prediction*. As the direct protection benefit rises, all relevant governments and the planner have a stronger reason to protect locally. Whether this greater benefit causes the *gap between decentralized provision and coordinated welfare maximization* to widen depends on the two firms' private decision margins.

In the exactly solved gamma=0 benchmark: both private margins endogenous -> gap slope +16/189; backup readiness frozen -> gap slope 0; location distribution frozen -> gap slope −16/1593. The global exact results reveal not just that the margins are present, but that removing either can qualitatively reverse the comparative-static effect. This is substantively more interesting than another proof of inefficient levels alone.

The positive slope persists on a nonempty gamma>0 interval. The model therefore makes a theoretically interpretable prediction about *strengthening* an existing protection incentive, not a universal claim about the absolute optimum of climate adaptation.

## Remaining caveats
- Direct local benefits mu are reduced-form, and the planner's industrial component is explicitly separated from this augmented social accounting. A microfoundation for the incidence and b could strengthen empirical relevance.
- With private location fixed, the government competition motive does not disappear entirely because jurisdiction shares only half of market surplus; the result does not imply that all forms of fiscal overinvestment vanish.
- The new results are exact in the stated model family. They do not estimate the expected magnitude of real climate risk, preparedness or fiscal competition.
- Under positive gamma, the continuity theorem proves an epsilon neighborhood but not a large empirically plausible subset. Clarifying this is important.

## Recommendation
**MAJOR R&R**, M1=5/5, M2=4/5, R1=4/5, W1=4/5. Correctly framed, the paper now has an identifiable and falsifiable qualitative theoretical prediction with rigorous equilibrium support, but publication would still require a disciplined contribution statement and careful proof and limitations presentation.
