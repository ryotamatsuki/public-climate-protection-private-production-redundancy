## 1. Precise target claim

At the separable-product rational witness
\[
(\gamma,q_0,k,H,b,c,\bar a)
=(0,3/10,2/25,1/100,11/200,10,2/25),
\quad 3/32<\mu<1/8,
\]
the first-stage policy instruments are **public primary-disruption risk reductions**. The planner coordinates $(a_A,a_B)$, preserving private firms' downstream decisions; governments choose their own $a_j$ noncooperatively. The marginal intervention $\mu$ is a **common direct public-benefit parameter**, entering the planner's payoff as $\mu(a_A+a_B)$ and each local government's payoff as $\mu a_j$.

Under fully re-solved private backup readiness and Bayesian primary location, the reported *symmetric* policies are
\[
a^P=(32\mu-3)/270,\quad a^N=(128\mu+5)/630,\quad
\frac{d}{d\mu}(a^N-a^P)=16/189>0.
\]
Under readiness fixed at the zero-protection private benchmark, re-solving location gives the gap slope **zero**. With primary locations fixed at $p=1/2$ and backup re-optimized, the gap slope is **$-16/1593<0$**. The mathematical certificates include global planner maximization and unilateral best responses, not simply on-path FOCs. A later, separate Lean certificate establishes *unique symmetric* policy Nash profiles, without ruling out *asymmetric* first-stage Nash equilibria. Positive $\gamma$ persistence and the separate Cournot substitution derivative are distinct claims.

**Exact comparative-static identity:** in the full $\gamma=0$ analytic family,
\[
a^P=(\mu-\mu_0)/t_c,\quad a^N=(\mu+m)/s,\quad
\frac{d(a^N-a^P)}{d\mu}
=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t_c}
=\frac{b-H}{16Hk\,s\,t_c}.
\]
Thus the sign condition **$b>H$** (with $s,t_c>0$) matters. There is no universal positive response law across all public-input models. This was independently re-evaluated using exact SymPy arithmetic on 2026-11-05. In the restricted games the slopes are zero and negative respectively. The strength of this result lies in the *mechanism-specific endogenous comparison*, not the difficultly of differentiating affine FOCs after their derivation.

## 2. Rules for claimed priority

To establish that a parent theorem **directly subsumes** the exact claim, one must identify a theorem (and its assumptions/primitive mapping) sufficient to cover **all** the following: (i) two governmental first-stage risk-reduction policy choices, (ii) privately financed backup/contingency readiness whose optimum varies with primary risk, (iii) endogenous primary-location response and hosting incentives, (iv) the planner-versus-decentralized policy-gap derivative with respect to a *common nonindustrial direct-benefit* parameter, and (v) both re-solved fixed-readiness and fixed-location counterfactual policy games generating the claimed three sign outcomes. Finding overprovision, protection-induced investment or public/private crowd-out alone does **not** subsume the interaction theorem. Conversely, the fact that another paper uses different notation, institutional context or policy variables does **not prove** conceptual originality; a general model or obvious corollary may still absorb it.

Evidence tiers:
- **A**: published full text, identified proposition/equations read;
- **B**: full working-paper version read (version differences must be noted);
- **C**: publisher/author abstract only or metadata, *not* theorem-level;
- **D**: inaccessible primary text; no conclusion about its proof.

## 4. Could the response interaction be an easy corollary of known comparative statics?

**Serious risk, not dismissed:** Once all three games have been algebraically reduced to symmetric policy FOCs affine in $\mu$, the response derivatives follow by elementary implicit differentiation. Thus **the three fractions themselves are not a high-level technical innovation**. A referee could argue that combining standard public-private substitution and location-attraction channels predicts the sign pattern; the unique-value condition $b>H$ may simply rearrange well-known marginal-benefit slopes. Lean verifies the equations but does not establish disciplinary originality or economic importance.

A successful priority defense needs to show that the *joint private-continuation equilibrium* imposes a **nontrivial restriction** on the response Jacobian or the planner–Nash curvature gap that is not an arbitrary calibration, and that the restricted games offer a theoretically meaningful mechanism test. The analytic primitive band and positive-$\gamma$ continuation help but may not by themselves address this criticism. The exact $\gamma$ derivative result, derived from full state-dependent Cournot equations, may provide a more defensible incremental insight; assess separately.

**Scope discipline:**
1. The $+\,0\,-$ comparison is proved for a *specific rational witness* and \(\mu\) interval; do not extrapolate all primitive regions.
2. A separate primitive sufficient-condition result derives the full-game slope sign $b>H$ (under positive curvature denominators), not an analogous general sign theorem for both ablations.
3. Existing crowd-out and generic local overprovision are **acknowledged prior art**.
4. The "requires joint endogenous operation" line describes the **specified restricted-games experiment**, not universal necessity for overprovision, or for every sign-changing fiscal comparative static.
5. Even full nonabsorption from the inaccessible Bayindir-Upmann and Walz–Wellisch published papers remains unknown.
