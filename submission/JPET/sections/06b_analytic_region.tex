\section{A Primitive Region and a Differentiated-Product Extension}
\label{sec:analytic-region}

The global certificates above establish the principal policy conflict at
explicit parameter vectors. The separable-product case also permits an
analytical sufficient-condition region. More importantly, this region
permits a continuation argument establishing a positive coordinated
optimum with decentralized overprovision for genuinely positive product
substitutability.

For $\gamma=0$, let $x=a_A$, $y=a_B$, $d=x-y$,
$q=q_0-(x+y)/2$, and set
\begin{equation}
U(q)=\frac{1}{32k}+\frac{(4k-q)(3k-q)}{128Hk^2},
\qquad h(q)=\frac{4k-q}{16k}.
\label{eq:analytic-U}
\end{equation}
Writing $t(q)=3(1-q)/8+q^2/(16k)$, the complete private
continuation induces the \emph{global} identities
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S(x,y)=2t(q)+U(q)d^2,\qquad
p(x,y)=\frac12+\frac{h(q)d}{2H}.
\label{eq:analytic-global}
\end{equation}
Let $Q=[q_0-\bar a,q_0]$, and define
\[
U_-=\min_Q U,\quad U_+=\max_Q U,\quad
D=\max_Q|U'|,\quad D_2=(64Hk^2)^{-1}.
\]
Also define
\begin{equation}
m=\frac{H(3k-q_0)+b(4k-q_0)}{16Hk},\quad
s=c-\frac{H+b}{16Hk},\quad
t_c=c-\frac1{8k},\quad
\mu_0=\frac{q_0-3k}{8k}.
\label{eq:analytic-parameters}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Global primitive conditions and benefit-induced overprovision]
\label{prop:global-primitive-band}
Suppose the following strict conditions hold:
\begin{align}
&3k<q_0<4k,\quad 0<\bar a<q_0,\quad U_->0,\quad
(4k-q_0+\bar a)\bar a<16kH,
\label{eq:analytic-band-a}\\
&c>\max\left\{
\frac{1}{8k}+4U_++2D\bar a+\frac{D_2\bar a^2}{2},
\frac12\left(\frac1{8k}+2U_++2D\bar a+
\frac{D_2\bar a^2}{4}\right)+\frac{b}{16Hk}
\right\},
\label{eq:analytic-band-c}\\
&m>0,\qquad 0<m/s<\bar a.
\label{eq:analytic-band-d}
\end{align}
Then the unaugmented game has a unique coordinated global optimum
$(0,0)$ and a strictly positive symmetric decentralized Nash
equilibrium $(m/s,m/s)$, with globally optimal unilateral responses
and unique interior private continuations throughout the policy square.

If additionally $b>H$ and
\begin{equation}
\mu_0<\mu<
\min\{\mu_0+t_c\bar a,\ s\bar a-m\},
\label{eq:analytic-mu-range}
\end{equation}
then the augmented-benefit game of
\eqref{eq:augmented-direct-benefit} has a strictly positive unique
coordinated optimum and a strictly larger symmetric Nash level,
both interior and globally incentive-compatible:
\begin{equation}
a^P=\frac{\mu-\mu_0}{t_c}
<a^N=\frac{\mu+m}{s}.
\label{eq:analytic-policy-pair}
\end{equation}
The overprovision gap increases with the directly valued benefit:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial(a^N-a^P)}{\partial\mu}
=\frac{b-H}{16Hk\,s\,t_c}>0.
\label{eq:analytic-gap-slope}
\end{equation}
This is a nonempty strict primitive region, containing the exact
rational benchmark \eqref{eq:zero-delta-witness} at $\mu=1/10$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:jpet-additional-proof-1}.


\subsection{An open region with positive product substitutability}

\begin{proposition}[Positive optimal protection and overprovision at $\gamma>0$]
\label{prop:positive-gamma-extension}
Fix the rational primitives of \eqref{eq:zero-delta-witness} and
let $\mu=1/10$. There exists $\epsilon>0$ such that for every
$\gamma\in(0,\epsilon)$, with product substitutability strictly
positive, the complete game admits unique interior private backup
and Bayesian location continuations for all first-stage policy
profiles. The augmented coordinated protection-policy planner has a
strictly positive, symmetric \emph{unique global optimum}
$a^P(\gamma)$. The decentralized game has a strictly positive
symmetric Nash equilibrium $a^N(\gamma)$, with globally optimal
unilateral responses, satisfying
\begin{equation}
0<a^P(\gamma)<a^N(\gamma)<\bar a.
\label{eq:positive-gamma-ranking}
\end{equation}
Both levels depend smoothly on $\gamma$ locally. On a possibly
smaller open neighborhood of $(\gamma,\mu)=(0,1/10)$ with
$\gamma>0$, the overprovision gap also increases with $\mu$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:jpet-additional-proof-2}.

The proposition is a genuine differentiated-product result,
but is deliberately local to a nonempty positive-$\gamma$
neighborhood; it does not assert the same augmented ranking
for all $\gamma\in(0,1)$ or for the original
$\gamma=12/25$ witness. The economic content is not that
generic fiscal competition or public--private substitution
is novel. Rather, the joint private-continuation game admits
a globally verified policy-ranking conflict and a
nontrivial benefit-dependent comparative static in a
primitive region that persists under interacting oligopolists.


\subsection{Which private response drives the benefit-gap comparative static?}

The positive derivative in
\eqref{eq:analytic-gap-slope} need not follow from
interjurisdictional competition alone. The two restricted
games in Proposition~\ref{prop:restricted-game-comparisons}
make it possible to separate the roles of backup and
primary-location responses while increasing the same
directly valued local benefit in both governments and the planner.

\begin{proposition}[A response-interaction test for the overprovision gap]
\label{prop:benefit-gap-response-interaction}
Hold the separable-product benchmark primitives at
\eqref{eq:zero-delta-witness}, and let
$3/32<\mu<1/8$. For each of the following
three games, augment every jurisdiction's payoff by
$\mu a_j$ and the planner's objective by
$\mu(a_A+a_B)$, holding the relevant restricted
private continuation fixed under all first-stage deviations.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item With both private backup readiness and primary-location
choice endogenous, the unique positive global coordinated
optimum and a positive symmetric globally incentive-compatible
local Nash equilibrium satisfy
\[
a^P=\frac{32\mu-3}{270},\qquad
a^N=\frac{128\mu+5}{630},\qquad
\frac{d(a^N-a^P)}{d\mu}=\frac{16}{189}>0.
\]
\item If readiness is held at the zero-protection
private level $r^*=15/16$ throughout the policy game,
while primary locations are re-solved, then
\[
a^{P,F}=\frac{\mu+3/128}{10},\qquad
a^{N,F}=\frac{\mu+25/256}{10},\qquad
\frac{d(a^{N,F}-a^{P,F})}{d\mu}=0.
\]
\item If the primary-location distribution is held at
$p=1/2$ throughout the policy game while backup
readiness is re-solved, then
\[
a^{P,L}=\frac{16}{135}\left(\mu-\frac3{32}\right),
\quad
a^{N,L}=\frac{32}{295}\left(\mu-\frac3{64}\right),
\quad
\frac{d(a^{N,L}-a^{P,L})}{d\mu}
=-\frac{16}{1593}<0.
\]
\end{enumerate}

All stated coordinated levels are unique global optima;
all local Nash levels are strictly positive, interior,
and globally optimal against unilateral deviations.
Thus, in this exact benchmark, the positive response of
the local overprovision gap to a common direct protection
benefit requires the joint endogenous operation of
readiness and location. This is a \emph{within-model
counterfactual necessity result}, not a claim that both
responses are necessary for overprovision in general.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:jpet-additional-proof-3}.

The contrasts clarify how firms' response margins
alter the benefit-gap comparative static. With
readiness fixed, location attraction remains a
motive for local protection, but the common direct
benefit raises decentralized and coordinated
provision at the same rate, leaving the gap
unchanged. With primary locations fixed,
unilateral protection cannot attract plants,
and coordinated provision responds more strongly
in this benchmark because local governments
internalize only part of industrial surplus.
When both private choices are endogenous,
protection changes backup readiness and thereby
the response of primary locations; under the
stated primitives, the decentralized policy level
rises faster than the coordinated optimum.
Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-gamma-extension}
establishes local persistence of this positive-gap
response for a nonempty set of
differentiated-product economies.


\subsection{Robustness to regional incidence and primary-plant-dependent hosting}
\label{sec:incidence-robustness}

The baseline divides industrial market surplus equally between
jurisdictions and values hosting an established primary plant
irrespective of disaster-state production. Both assumptions can
be relaxed locally without changing private production decisions.
Let
\begin{equation}
\theta_A(p)=\frac12+\eta\left(p-\frac12\right),
\qquad \theta_B(p)=1-\theta_A(p),
\label{eq:endogenous-incidence}
\end{equation}
where $|\eta|<1$ ensures both shares are nonnegative whenever
$p\in[0,1]$. Positive $\eta$ reallocates more industrial surplus
to the jurisdiction attracting primary facilities. Separately,
let $\rho\in[0,1]$ denote the fraction of the location-attached
benefit paid only if the primary plant in the hosting jurisdiction is operational.

The probabilities that a hosted \emph{primary} plant operates after
the localized disaster are simply
\begin{equation}
v_A=1-q_A,\qquad v_B=1-q_B.
\label{eq:availability-conditional}
\end{equation}
Thus the availability-contingent hosting return accrues from
locally operating primary facilities, not from geographically
separate backup production. No additional private action or
correlation assumption is introduced.

The local objectives become
\begin{align}
G_A^{\eta,\rho}
 &=\theta_A(p)\mathcal S
   +2bp[(1-\rho)+\rho v_A]
   -\frac{c}{2}a_A^2+\mu a_A,\\
G_B^{\eta,\rho}
 &=\theta_B(p)\mathcal S
   +2b(1-p)[(1-\rho)+\rho v_B]
   -\frac{c}{2}a_B^2+\mu a_B,
\label{eq:perturbed-regional-payoffs}
\end{align}
and the coordinated planner continues to maximize their sum
$W^{\rho}=G_A^{\eta,\rho}+G_B^{\eta,\rho}$.
The planner's objective is independent of $\eta$ by construction
but now values expected primary-operation-related hosting benefits
when $\rho>0$. The original model is exactly the case
$(\eta,\rho)=(0,0)$.

\begin{proposition}[Robustness to two economic incidence assumptions]
\label{prop:incidence-robustness}
Fix $(q_0,k,H,b,c,\bar a)=(3/10,2/25,1/100,11/200,10,2/25)$,
and take $(\gamma,\eta,\rho,\mu)=(0,0,0,1/10)$
as the reference point.
There exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that for every
\[
0\le\gamma<\varepsilon,\quad
|\eta|<\varepsilon,\quad
0\le\rho<\varepsilon,\quad
|\mu-1/10|<\varepsilon
\]
within the maintained feasible parameter domain, the
perturbed game has unique interior firm-level backup and
Bayesian location continuations at every public-policy profile.
Its coordinated policy optimum is unique, symmetric, strictly
positive, and interior. Its decentralized policy game has a
strictly positive symmetric Nash equilibrium at which each
jurisdiction's first-stage policy choice is a \emph{global}
best response; that Nash provision strictly exceeds the
coordinated level. Moreover, the overprovision gap continues
to increase strictly with the common direct protection benefit
$\mu$ throughout this neighborhood.

At the separable-product reference point
$(\gamma,\rho,\eta)=(0,0,0)$, the effect of strengthening
the location-dependence of regional industrial surplus incidence
is strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
\left.
\frac{\partial a^N}{\partial\eta}
\right|_{\mu=1/10,\,\gamma=\rho=\eta=0}
=\frac{t(q^N)h(q^N)}{H s}
=\frac{19956004}{78764805}>0,
\qquad q^N=q_0-\frac{89}{3150}.
\label{eq:incidence-response}
\end{equation}
This is an equilibrium comparative static, not just an
on-path marginal effect.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:jpet-additional-proof-4}.

The robustness claim remains local: it does not deliver
a global characterization for large reallocations of
industrial surplus or fully availability-contingent hosting.
It does establish that neither an exactly fixed half-share
incidence rule nor fully state-independent hosting is
necessary for the positive-optimum policy ranking or
benefit-gap comparative static in an open region of
differentiated-product economies.


\subsection{Does stronger product competition amplify the policy distortion?}
\label{sec:product-market-amplification}

The continuity theorem establishes that the new overprovision results are
not confined to independent products, but does not sign the
\emph{response to product substitutability}. The full
location-profile and disaster-state continuation permits an
exact directional comparison at $\gamma=0$.

\begin{proposition}[Product competition can amplify local overprovision]
\label{prop:gamma-gap-amplification}
Maintain the rational protection and private-investment primitives
of Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-gamma-extension}, and set
$\mu=1/10$ with the baseline incidence and hosting rules
$(\eta,\rho)=(0,0)$. Let $a^P(\gamma)$ and
$a^N(\gamma)$ denote the unique coordinated optimum and the
symmetric decentralized equilibrium obtained by smooth continuation
for sufficiently small nonnegative $\gamma$.
At the separable-product reference point,
\begin{align}
\left.\frac{d a^P}{d\gamma}\right|_{0^+}
&=\frac{29820457}{354294000}>0,\\
\left.\frac{d a^N}{d\gamma}\right|_{0^+}
&=\frac{3355337640241}{15630875552250}>0,\\
\left.\frac{d(a^N-a^P)}{d\gamma}\right|_{0^+}
&=\frac{16317660285149}{125047004418000}>0.
\label{eq:gamma-gap-amplification}
\end{align}
Consequently, for sufficiently small positive product
substitutability, stronger product-market competition raises
both protection levels but increases decentralized provision
more, strictly magnifying the local overprovision gap.
This is a local comparative static at the stated primitives,
not a monotonicity claim over $0<\gamma<1$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} See Appendix~\ref{app:jpet-additional-proof-5}.

The result separates two effects that are sometimes conflated:
increasing product substitutability reduces the level of
symmetric contingency readiness under the maintained interior
private equilibrium, but that fact alone does not sign the
\emph{government-policy} response. When the disaster-state
market payoffs, correlated-failure contingencies, location cutoffs,
and the value of backup reliability are all differentiated,
the public planner's own protection also rises. The local
government's stronger response is an additional equilibrium
effect of mobile-plant competition. The exact derivative
in \eqref{eq:gamma-gap-amplification} gives the full-game
policy implication that the separable-product model alone
could not establish.
